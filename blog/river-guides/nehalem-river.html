NEHALEM RIVER · OREGON

Nehalem River and Bay Fishing

Updated November 21, 2026 · 2 min read
Photo: Paul Cole from Portland, Oregon, USA, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons
The Nehalem is the biggest of the north coast rivers and the one most tied to its bay. Fall salmon fishing centers on the bay and tidewater, while its North Fork is a hatchery river with fall coho and winter steelhead.

Runs through the year

  • •
    Fall Chinook: mostly a bay and lower-tidewater fishery, trolled with spinners, with float fishing in upper tidewater.
  • •
    Coho: wild and hatchery coho reach the bay in late September and October. North Fork hatchery coho run into early or mid December.
  • •
    Winter steelhead: the North Fork's hatchery steelhead run from December through April.
  • •
    Sea-run cutthroat: they fill the bay and tidewater in summer.

Reading the water

This is a rain-fed river with no dam to steady it. It blows out after heavy rain and drops back into shape over a few days. The best fishing is usually on a dropping river that's turning from muddy to green, after fresh fish have moved in on the rise. When the big rivers are too high, smaller rivers and tributaries clear first. Watch the USGS gauge after a storm and plan your trip for the drop.

Methods that work on the north coast

  • •
    Winter steelhead: side-drifting from a boat, bobber-dogging, and a float and jig from the bank, with spinners as the water drops. Small cured roe clusters, sand shrimp, Corkies and yarn, beads under a float, and pink or white marabou and rubber jigs are the standards. Fly anglers swing sink-tips with Intruder-style flies and egg-sucking leeches.
  • •
    Fall Chinook: trolled spinners and herring in the bay and lower tidewater, cured eggs under a float in upper tidewater, and plugs or back-bounced bait in the river.
  • •
    Spring Chinook: bay and tidewater early in the run, then the river's deeper holes in May and June, with eggs and shrimp under a float, plugs, or trolled herring.
  • •
    Coho: spinners, twitched jigs and eggs in the fall.
  • •
    Sea-run cutthroat: small spinners, small baitfish streamers and wet flies in the bay, tidewater and lower river from midsummer.

Gear

For steelhead, a spinning or casting rod of 8.5 to 10.5 feet for 8 to 12 pound line, or a 7 or 8 weight fly rod (a spey rod helps on the bigger water). For Chinook, step up to a heavier casting rod for 20 to 30 pound line.

Be a good guest

A steelhead or salmon with its adipose fin intact is a wild fish. Keep it in the water when you release it, use barbless hooks, and keep the fight short. Winter rivers rise fast and carry logs: never wade a river that's rising and colored.

Have more questions?

Seasons, limits and gear rules change, sometimes in the middle of a season. Before you go, read ODFW's regulation updates and the Northwest Zone fishing page.
